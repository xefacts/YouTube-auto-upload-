## Evidence audit and uncertainties

- **Invitation, Sunday Children's Day program, June 9/10, 1912, Villisca and overnight guests:** [Iowa State Capitol Tour Guides, *Pieces of Iowa's Past* (2015)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/publications/TB/663533.pdf); [Des Moines Register (2022, updated 2025)](https://www.desmoinesregister.com/story/news/2022/06/09/1912-villisca-ax-murders-what-to-know-iowa-cold-case/7567590001/); [Smithsonian historical essay](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-ax-murderer-who-got-away-117037374/).
- **Mary Peckham notices lack of routine and knocks; family of six plus two girls found killed:** Iowa legislative historical account and Des Moines Register. Des Moines Register has some conflicting retold details about who opened a door, so the script makes no door-entry or locked-room claim.
- **Family ax left in guest room:** Iowa's legislative historical account explicitly places it in the guest room; [Iowa Cold Cases](https://iowacoldcases.org/case-summaries/villisca-axe-murders/) independently places the ax in the downstairs room with the guests. No physical proof of a specific killer is inferred from its location.
- **Bloodhounds used without finding a suspect:** Smithsonian says bloodhounds were tried without success; Des Moines Register reports dogs sent to hunt for a suspect. The script does not claim the dogs followed a particular trail.
- **Townspeople entering the home after discovery, disturbing possible evidence:** Smithsonian describes substantial visitor traffic and scattered fingerprints; [Newspapers.com historical topic summary](https://www.newspapers.com/topics/crimes-mysteries/villisca-axe-murders/) also reports crowd contamination. Do not claim this single issue alone caused the crime to go unsolved.
- **Minister Lyn George Jacklin Kelly's 1917 confession after lengthy questioning, retraction, first hung jury and second acquittal:** Iowa historical account and Des Moines Register both support this sequence. The narration uses “minister George Kelly,” not “the killer.” Smithsonian discusses the confession and recantation but has a differing characterization of some court panels; avoid details beyond the locally documented outcomes.
- **Eight victims and ongoing unsolved status:** Iowa Capitol Tour Guides and Des Moines Register. “No proven killer” means no offender was legally established; it does not erase the historical investigation. The script does not claim a living suspect exists today.
- **Deliberate exclusions:** no asserted motive, attacker identity/gender/number, suspect hiding in the attic, covered-mirror significance, exact guest ages, haunted-house theory, or fake quote. Secondary accounts disagree about minor scene details; nothing speculative is framed as fact.
