Fictional detective host: Ben. Working channel name **Ben’s Casebook**, proposed matching `@BensCasebook` **unverified / unreserved**.

## Source and verification log

1. Royal Museums Greenwich, curator Andrew Choong Han Lin, [The mystery of the Mary Celeste](https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/maritime-history/mystery-mary-celeste): the Dei Gratia sighted the Mary Celeste near the Azores in December 1872, the ship was deserted, the boat was missing, belongings appeared intact, and a pump was partly dismantled. The fate of all aboard is unresolved.
2. Smithsonian Magazine, [Abandoned Ship: The Mary Celeste](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/abandoned-ship-the-mary-celeste-174488104/): independently corroborates the 1872 discovery, ten persons aboard, belongings, missing boat, dismantled pump, later salvage voyage to Gibraltar, no proven foul play. A captain's fear of foundering and decision to abandon ship is **a reconstruction/theory**, not established fact.
3. The sources differ on the sighting day (December 4 vs December 5). **Avoid a precise day**; use only 1872 and "near the Azores." Do not depict the crew's actual fate, claim pirates or supernatural causes, assert the vessel was about to sink, or treat a hypothetical abandonment as proven.

## Final spoken script (~74 words; separate real timing check required)

>
> I'm Ben. This is Ben's Casebook. Let's follow the clues.
>
> In 1872, sailors boarded the Mary Celeste. Ten people were missing, but their belongings remained. The lifeboat was gone. One pump was partly dismantled. Had Captain Briggs feared the ship might sink and ordered an escape? That's a theory, not a solved case. The ship reached port. None of the ten people did.

The final sentence is complete and factually responsible; do not mutilate it to engineer a false loop. Hook precedes the repeated host line. Opening phrase changes for each episode, **Ben's signature line does not**.
