# Florida Keys — private geography film V2

**This is a new 58.53-second, narration-led film, not the earlier caption-only 36-second study and not the Florida mystery episode.** Master: `FLORIDA_KEYS_GEOGRAPHY_V2_VOICED_1080x1920.mp4`, H.264 1080×1920 / 30 fps (1,756 frames) + 48 kHz stereo AAC. Integrated loudness −17.5 LUFS, true peak −3.7 dBFS. All 1,756 frames decoded. No reference footage, frames, narrator, graphics, transcript or map were copied into the new master. It was uploaded owner-only for review, not to YouTube.

## Reference reverse-engineering and editorial decisions

The five reference geography shorts were reviewed again on a contact sheet and at selected full-resolution frames. Their clips are ~60 s / 576×1024. `Who Celebrates New Year First` alternates wide relief map, glowing date-line, localized islands, graphic callouts and dramatic scale jumps every roughly 3–5 s. `The Darien Gap` alternates a wide map, yellow draw-on route, green selected area, distance arrows, terrain insert and icon/cutout shots. `Why Animals Don't Cross This Invisible Border` uses a glowing pink segmented trace with small independent animal cutouts and wide/local re-frames. `The Distance Between the US and Russia` rapidly re-frames global context, island closeup, comparison graphics and time-boundary beats. `Why Do Time Zones Look So Strange` interleaves map-wide grid, location close-ups, exact lines and labels. The editorial takeaways: ~3 s average new information beat; fast cuts or 0.2–0.4 s map-scale dissolves, never 25 s on one wide map; parallax clouds distinct from land; localized glow, accurate visual hierarchy, large simple headings and small lower voice-synced captions. This is a method/style analysis—not an extraction or reuse of their native editing files, voices or words.

The V2 original cut has **18 voice-driven beats** and four primary visual scales: software-UV-projected rotating Earth; stylized regional relief map; separately painted Keys macro relief; separately illustrated reef/limestone cutaway; plus a bridge aerial. Earth/cloud layers rotate separately. Map-region glow, multi-stage route, coral/limestone annotation, waterline, island nodes and bridge trace are independent code-drawn layers with animated opacities, draw-on and camera transforms. Map-scale clouds drift independently. Audio has an actual generated male English narration from a locally obtained open voice model, original short phrase captions paced to its segments, real recorded sea and wind and editorial transition gusts. No reference voice was cloned or used.

## Original script and timing

The exact 18-line original spoken script and scene mapping live in `render_geography_v2.py` (`BEATS`). Generated start/end times are in `render_cache/v2_timing.json` and included with the source pack; the pacing is driven by the synthesized waveforms, not by a fixed 5-second caption loop. The video answers a specific question: why the Florida Keys form an island chain. The interpretation distinguishes the upper keys' ancient fossil reef from lower islands' limestone/sand. Geological claims were checked against [NOAA's Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary habitat explanation](https://floridakeys.noaa.gov/education/habitats.html) and [NPS Everglades geology](https://www.nps.gov/ever/learn/nature/evergeology.htm). NPS notes the upper keys' reef remnants, lower keys' carbonate sands/muds, and sea-level variation; [NOAA's Keys podcast](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/podcast/sep23/nop68-florida-keys.html) describes the island chain extending beyond Key West. The road is described as connecting **many** islands, not every island, and there is no made-up mileage.

## Rebuild and material package

Install Python 3.11+ with `pillow numpy opencv-python scipy imageio-ffmpeg piper-tts`; set `work/tts_models/en_US-libritts_r-medium.onnx` and adjacent `.onnx.json` to the separately provided voice model. From the repository root run `python new_channel_detective_timeglass/train2_geography_study/render_geography_v2.py`. The code synthesizes spoken lines and derives exact times into ignored `render_cache/`; it mixes the original voice, waves/wind and directly encodes the video. Dependencies of the original `render_geography_full.py` / `build_study.py` are shared. For editorial re-use, the private `V2_EDITING_SOURCE_AND_ASSETS.zip` contains the Python source, modular PNG layers, backplates, credits, timing JSON, raw voice WAV and final mixed WAV; the 75 MB voice model has a separate private Drive file. The user's nine reference MP4s themselves are not included in the source pack and remain in their source folder.
