## Fictional intro story — “The torn file”

At a rain-dark archive crime scene, an empty file drawer sits open. A ripped case file, a key, and a faint print lie on the desk; a yellow marker indicates evidence. The detective raises his Prism magnifier. A fine torn fiber comes into focus, implying somebody moved or reassembled the document. He does **not** solve the crime in the intro: the detail opens a question for the episode. This is an **invented crime-scene motif**, not a historical reconstruction or a claim about a real case.

**Recommended 4.8-second intro, after a factual episode hook (if used in a history Short):**

| Time | Visual beat | Sound / spoken line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.0–0.9s | Rain against archive window; quick close-up of open drawer and torn file. | Low room tone; distant rain. No music. |
| 0.9–2.2s | New generated pose of the detective steps beside desk; picks up the lens. | Soft shoe step, paper shift, tiny metal click. |
| 2.2–3.5s | Macro through the lens: paper fiber and faint impression come into focus. The cyan prism glint crosses only the glass. | Controlled focus click. Detective (US English): **“Someone moved the evidence.”** Start at ~2.5s. |
| 3.5–4.8s | Quick determined glance, lens wipes frame into the episode's first case image; optional small provisional “CASE OPEN” type. | Short airy transition; silence under the next story line. |

The final dialogue timing should be checked against the actual voice performance. Do not insert a fabricated crime-scene event as historical fact. No name or handle is spoken until the user chooses one.
