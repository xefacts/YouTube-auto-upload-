## Repeatable high-level traits visible in the supplied examples

- Vertical, short-form explainer format; the supplied examples are about 39–65 seconds, not 19 minutes.
- A narrated curiosity hook, then one surprising mechanism, historical detail, or counterintuitive fact, followed by a payoff.
- Simple recurring cartoon people (round pale faces, bold outlines, small expressive brows/mouths, minimal bodies) staged inside richly colored, depth-rich 3D-looking environments. The overall appearance is 2D/2.5D characters over 3D-style sets, not realistic human character animation.
- The environment illustrates the exact idea being narrated: ships and cannonballs, castles and artifacts, Japanese workplaces/venues, rail infrastructure, sleep/brain concepts.
- Frequent scene/location changes. The samples use bright, saturated storybook lighting and clear silhouettes; darker scenes are reserved for suspense beats.
- Large bold white captions with a dark stroke near the bottom, often only a short phrase or one/two words at a time.
- The sampled frames show changing facial expressions, but sparse stills cannot establish the accuracy of mouth visemes against each syllable. A human listen-through is still required for voice tone, pace, pronunciation and dialogue timing.

## Original adaptation recommendation

For UNCASE, use this broad production grammar—not copied scripts, distinctive characters, shot compositions, artwork, or voice: an original mascot/host, high-contrast illustrative character animation in original 3D sets, quick narration-synced scene changes, readable karaoke/phrase captions, and a sourced true-crime story. Keep facts and uncertainty distinct, avoid graphic material, and do not imply guilt by unconvicted living people.

## Runtime decision needed

The provided examples support roughly 40–65 seconds. A 19:20 video would be a separate long-form production, requiring a much longer original script, many more scenes, extended asset/render time, and a different pacing plan. Confirm which deliverable is intended before committing to the full render.
