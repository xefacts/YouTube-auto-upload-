## Claim-level evidence

### 01 — New Coke (1985)
- New formula preferred in large blind taste tests; launch/replacement on April 23, 1985: Coca-Cola's history and HISTORY's chronology.
- Complaints flooded company phones and protest groups appeared: Coca-Cola's history; HISTORY describes call and letter volumes. This is not a claim that every customer objected.
- Original formula returned July 11, **79 days** later as Coca-Cola Classic; New Coke continued alongside it: Coca-Cola explicitly states side-by-side sales; HISTORY corroborates date/return. The script deliberately does **not** say New Coke was immediately discontinued.
- https://www.coca-colacompany.com/about-us/history/new-coke-the-most-memorable-marketing-blunder-ever
- https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/april-23/new-coke-debuts-one-of-the-biggest-product-flops-in-history

### 02 — Carrington Event (1859)
- Auroras visible as far south as Cuba; red/green/purple sky; telegraph systems went haywire, sparked, shocked operators and ignited telegraph paper: NASA's Sun overview.
- Independent institutional corroboration of auroras, telegraph disruption and fires caused by sparking: UK Met Office historical summary.
- The observed September 1 solar flare preceded the brilliant auroras before dawn the next day; the script avoids claiming the auroras preceded the telegraph disruption. It does **not** claim 1859 had satellites, modern blackouts, or worldwide fire in every office.
- https://solarsystem.nasa.gov/solar-system/sun/in-depth.amp
- https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/blog/2026/new-guide-helps-transport-sector-prepare-for-severe-space-weather

### 03 — St. Louis Olympic marathon (1904)
- Fred Lorz crossed first to cheers, but **after dropping out** had ridden in an automobile, then run the last stretch; he was disqualified. Thomas Hicks was the actual winner: International Olympic Committee (IOC) account and Smithsonian history.
- Hicks's handlers gave him strychnine; it was used as a stimulant and was not prohibited by the race rules then. The script does not call Hicks a disqualified cheat or say Lorz rode the entire distance.
- https://www.olympics.com/en/news/the-strange-case-of-the-st-louis-1904-marathon
- https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/how-the-1904-marathon-became-one-of-the-weirdest-olympic-events-of-all-time-14910747/

### 04 — Mona Lisa theft (1911)
- Painting was stolen from the Louvre on Monday, August 21, 1911; former Louvre glazier Vincenzo Peruggia tried to sell it to an Italian dealer after more than two years; dealer alerted authorities and it was recovered: the Louvre's institutional account.
- Smithsonian's detailed history independently supports the date, long disappearance, former glazier and recovery after attempted sale. The script does **not** claim visitors noticed its absence on Monday: the museum was closed to visitors that day, and Smithsonian says it took about 26 hours to notice.
- https://www.louvre.fr/en/explore/the-palace/from-the-mona-lisa-to-the-wedding-feast-at-cana
- https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/stolen-how-the-mona-lisa-became-the-worlds-most-famous-painting-16406234/
