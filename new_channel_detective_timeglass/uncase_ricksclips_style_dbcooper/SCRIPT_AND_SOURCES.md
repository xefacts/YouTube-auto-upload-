## Narration

One hijacker vanished with two hundred thousand dollars. Years later, only fifty-eight hundred came back.

On November twenty-fourth, nineteen seventy-one, a man using the name Dan Cooper boarded Northwest Orient Flight Three-O-Five. The press later called him D.B. He claimed his briefcase held a bomb and demanded four parachutes and two hundred thousand dollars.

In Seattle, he released all thirty-six passengers, kept the crew aboard, and ordered the plane south. Somewhere between Seattle and Reno, Cooper lowered the rear stairs and jumped into a stormy night.

The FBI considered more than eight hundred suspects—but never proved who he was. Then, in nineteen eighty, a boy found fifty-eight hundred dollars from the ransom. Serial numbers matched. But the money did not show where Cooper landed, or whether he survived.

After forty-five years, the FBI redirected its resources. The identity is still unknown. Did Cooper die in the jump—or escape? The evidence cannot settle it.

**Fact-check caveat:** Saying the case was “closed” would be misleading: the FBI ended active resource allocation in 2016; it did not identify the hijacker or prove his fate. The $5,800 was matched to ransom by serial numbers, but that does not establish who placed it at the river site or what happened to Cooper.
