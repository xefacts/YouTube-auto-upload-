# Video 01 — provisional production plan

**Status:** Planning/review plan; not approval to publish.
**Working assumption:** Use the existing D.B. Cooper draft as the first episode. If the intended first topic is different, change the topic before making a new render.

## Creative brief

- **Working title:** *The $5,800 clue that couldn't solve D.B. Cooper*
- **Format:** YouTube Short, vertical 9:16, 1080×1920 delivery, 30 fps.
- **Current cut:** 65.5 seconds; 15 planned shots; current private-review render is separate from public release.
- **Audience promise:** Explain how a small part of a ransom reappeared years later—and why that evidence still did not answer who Cooper was or what happened to him.
- **Story question:** What did the recovered $5,800 prove, and what did it leave unresolved?
- **Tone:** Curious, brisk, evidence-led, lightly suspenseful; no invented certainty or sensationalized real-person portrayal.
- **Reference treatment:** Learn only broad mechanics—immediate story question, clear narration, lively original 3D scenes, varied shot scale, purposeful transitions, legible captions, and a final unresolved question. Do not duplicate any creator's dialogue, characters, logo, shot order, framing, voice, or footage.

## Script and fact guardrails

- Keep the current FBI and National Archives sources in `SCRIPT_AND_SOURCES.md` attached to relevant claims.
- Distinguish established facts from inference: the ransom serial numbers matched; that alone does **not** establish who placed the money at the river site, where Cooper landed, or whether he survived.
- Describe 2016 accurately: the FBI redirected investigative resources; do not say it solved or conclusively closed the case.
- Present all people and sets as illustrative reconstructions. Do not portray a specific real person as a definitive likeness of the unidentified hijacker.
- A fact-checker should verify every exact number/date against the cited primary sources before final release.

## Caption, sound, and edit rules

- Burn in synchronized captions, as requested. Keep them within mobile safe areas, normally one or two short lines, with emphasis only on spoken key terms.
- Time picture changes to narration, but vary shot duration with information density; allow a short hold on the $5,800 reveal and final uncertainty.
- Use code-built 3D sets and character animation, not a still-image slideshow. Maintain consistent character, lighting direction, screen direction, and map orientation.
- Preserve the approved voice recording. Keep music/SFX subordinate and follow the supplied kit's mix guidance; no clipping or startling effect over a factual reveal.
