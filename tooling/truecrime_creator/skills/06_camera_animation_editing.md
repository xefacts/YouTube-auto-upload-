# Skill 06 — Camera, transitions and hybrid 2D/3D editing

## Shot grammar
- Begin on an image with a readable focal point; establish composition before moving.
- Use purposeful push-in for a clue, pull-back for context, lateral move to reveal evidence, and map travel for geography.
- Layer background / subject / foreground overlays at distinct parallax rates; keep motion subtle and avoid fake depth that changes evidence.
- Use real 3D camera moves only when a modeled scene exists. A 2D image with animated crop and overlays is 2.5D, not fully rigged 3D; label it honestly.
- Cut on a story turn, voice phrase or change of evidence. Use match cuts, motivated wipes, map-line reveals and restrained dissolves. Avoid random glitch transitions and zoom spam.
- Design captions in safe zones at phone size; one short phrase per beat, contrast tested, never obscure the key clue.
- Sound design supports, never overwhelms, narration. Build original procedural sounds or use separately licensed CC0 material with documented credit.

## Project tool routing
- Pillow/OpenCV/FFmpeg/MoviePy for fast, reproducible editorial animation and QA.
- Remotion for React-authored motion systems if license eligibility is confirmed.
- OpenFilm for HTML/CSS/SVG timeline experiments when browser renderer is available.
- Blender for genuine 3D blocking/rendering if installed and performance permits; not installed in this sandbox. No native 8K claim from upscaling.
- Manim is useful for diagrams/technical overlays, not a shortcut for human character animation; dependency-heavy and not installed here.

## Frame-by-frame review
Render contact sheets at scene starts, transition midpoints, every 2–3 seconds, and final word. Check focal continuity, asset rights, geography, labels, safe margins, actual camera motion, accidental freezes/black frames and audio/caption sync. Use automated detection as an assist, not a substitute for playback.
