# Skill 02 — Evidence, attribution, and source discipline

1. Build a claim ledger before drafting: exact claim; source URL/title/date; primary vs secondary; direct quotation/page/timecode; confidence; caveat; on-screen treatment.
2. For central facts, seek one primary record and at least one independent reputable secondary source. If a primary record is unavailable, use multiple independent institutional/reporting sources and disclose the limitation.
3. Separate: **established fact**, **prosecutor allegation**, **defendant claim**, **witness recollection**, **inference**, and **unknown**. Attribute each explicitly in narration.
4. Read beyond search snippets. Check the underlying source and date; note corrections, appeal history, jurisdiction and exact disposition.
5. Preserve uncertainty. “Unsolved” means no official resolution; it does not mean the internet can identify the culprit. Do not name living private suspects or repeat unsupported accusations.
6. Avoid false precision when sources conflict; use ranges or omit the detail. Never invent a quote, clue, forensic result, confession, sentence, or police action.
7. Give every episode a compact source list. Retain a private claim ledger and recheck it before publication.
