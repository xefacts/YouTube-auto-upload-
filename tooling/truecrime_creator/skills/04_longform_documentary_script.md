# Skill 04 — Long-form crime documentary structure

## Research-first outline (10–25 min adaptable)
1. Cold open with a verified moment, object or contradiction; do not fabricate dialogue.
2. State the question and scope. Tell viewers what sources are available and what the film cannot establish.
3. Introduce people with dates/roles and victim context; avoid reducing a person to a plot device.
4. Establish chronology with source citations, maps and location context.
5. Present evidence in sequence; identify who collected it, when, limitations and what it can/cannot prove.
6. Show competing explanations fairly, strongest evidence for/against, and where testimony conflicts.
7. Explain investigation and court record, including acquittals, appeals and procedural limits.
8. Resolve the question only as far as records allow; separate conviction from factual certainty where relevant.
9. Give current case status and family/community context with care.
10. End on an evidence-grounded takeaway, corrections note and full sources.

## Long-form pacing
Build act turns from genuine discoveries or procedural consequences; refresh context every few minutes, not with manufactured cliffhangers. Use chapters, maps, timeline cards and brief recaps after complex sequences. Draft VO first, read aloud, then storyboard. Aim for a complete legal/factual review before visual polishing.

## Required inserts
Claim ledger; source notes by scene; subject map; chronology; uncertainty box; permissions/licensing register; names/pronunciation guide; victim/privacy sensitivity check; legal disposition; correction log; transcript/captions.
