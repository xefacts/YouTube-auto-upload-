# Skill 07 — Responsible true-crime storytelling

- Treat victims and families as people, not hooks or thumbnails. Avoid gratuitous injury detail, private addresses, graphic imagery and identifiable minors.
- Avoid accusing any living person absent reliable official findings; clearly state acquittals, appeals, disputed claims and unresolved status.
- Never fabricate archival footage, interrogation audio, witness quotes, evidence photos or official-looking documents. Label artistic reconstructions both on-screen and in description.
- Do not call a theory “the answer.” For open cases, state that no public official resolution has been established and do not mobilize viewers to contact suspects/families.
- Avoid interfering with active investigations, doxxing, harassment, vigilantism or disclosure of sensitive personal data.
- Sensitivity-check title, thumbnail, copy, VO and soundtrack. Factual and non-graphic can still be dramatic through uncertainty, timelines, objects and investigative reversals.
- Correct errors visibly. Preserve a record of changes.
