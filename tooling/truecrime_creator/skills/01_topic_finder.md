# Skill 01 — Pick a true-crime topic with a real story engine

## Objective
Find a compelling, sourceable case that can be responsibly told—not a sensational headline dressed up as research.

## Topic score (0–5 each; require 24/35, no fatal ethics/evidence concern)
1. **Evidence depth:** primary records, court judgments, official archives or multiple independent reputable reports.
2. **Story engine:** a clear contradiction, overlooked detail, failed assumption, turning point or evidence-led reveal.
3. **Resolution honesty:** can explain what is known, what was decided, and what remains unresolved.
4. **Visual potential:** original maps, timelines, objects, locations, diagrams or licensed public-domain material.
5. **Audience value:** a useful procedural insight, historical context, or dignified way to remember the victim.
6. **Short-form shape:** one core question with 2–4 meaningful beats; not too many names and dates.
7. **Brand fit:** unsolved crimes/cold cases, evidence-led, non-graphic, compatible with responsible sponsors.

Reject topics when facts hinge on rumor, victim privacy risk outweighs public interest, there is a current active investigation that could be harmed, the main allegation is disputed but cannot be fairly represented, or the only available imagery is copyrighted and unauthorized.

## Topic-mining sources to search manually or through permitted APIs
- Library of Congress Chronicling America (historical U.S. newspapers): https://www.loc.gov/newspapers/
- National Archives Catalog (U.S. records): https://catalog.archives.gov/
- State/county court and archive catalogs for the relevant jurisdiction.
- Court opinions and official prosecution/police releases; distinguish allegations from findings.
- Smithsonian, university archives, museums and reputable public broadcasters for context.
- GDELT can surface news trends, not verify facts: https://www.gdeltproject.org/ . Confirm every claim elsewhere.
- YouTube search/Studio trends can indicate audience interest, not truth or handle availability.

## Topic-finder query patterns
`site:archives.gov [case/person/date]`; `site:loc.gov [case] newspaper`; `[case] court judgment PDF`; `[case] police official statement`; `"[person]" trial archive`; then search independently for conflicting testimony and corrections.

Log candidates in a CSV with case, jurisdiction, date, status, victim dignity/privacy risks, score, primary source, independent source, visual rights, next verification, and editor decision. Never infer that a missing search result proves no record exists.
