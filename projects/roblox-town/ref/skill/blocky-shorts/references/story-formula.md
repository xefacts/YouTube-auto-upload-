# Writing the script — the "In this town…" formula

These shorts work because the writing is tight. A good script carries a mediocre animation; a great animation can't save a flat script. Spend real effort here and show the user the script before anything gets rendered.

## The shape (20–35 seconds, 60–110 words)
1. **Rule hook (0–3 s).** One absurd rule of the world, stated in the first sentence. "In this town, when you yawn… day turns to night." The viewer has to understand the world in a single line.
2. **The exploit (3–5 s).** One character finds a way to use the rule. "One guy found a cheat code."
3. **Rule of three (5–10 s).** Three fast mini-scenes, each a different setting, same pattern, escalating. "Pop quiz? Yawn. School's over. / Chores? Yawn. Bedtime. / Dentist? Yawn. Closed." Each beat = one shot, 1–2 seconds.
4. **The turn (10–12 s).** "But he forgot one thing." The rule has a side effect.
5. **Chaos (12–16 s).** The side effect spreads. Show it visually (flicker, crowds, animals reacting). "Day. Night. Day. Night. The roosters gave up."
6. **Authority reacts (16–19 s).** Someone in charge makes a law, a ban, a rule. "So the mayor made a new law: No. More. Yawning."
7. **Tension (19–22 s).** Everyone obeys and suffers for it. "Everyone held it in. For one whole week."
8. **Trigger (22–25 s).** The most mundane possible thing breaks it. "Then the teacher started a lecture… on fractions."
9. **Payoff + callback button (25–30 s).** The rule fires at maximum scale, then a dry final line that calls back to an earlier character or setting. "The whole town yawned at once. That was three years ago. He's still talking."

Not every story needs all nine beats. Hook → exploit → three → turn → payoff → button is the minimum.

## Line rules (lines = captions = timing)
- Split narration into **caption chunks of 1–3 words**, cut where a speaker would naturally breathe or where the joke turns: `"In this town,"`, `"when you"`, `"*yawn...*"`.
- **Highlight** the word the joke hinges on with `*...*`: the rule word, the punchline, the twist. About one highlight per shot, never more than two.
- **Short sentences.** Deadpan. No adjectives you don't need. Fragments are great: "Pop quiz? Yawn. School's over."
- **Write for the ear:** ellipses (`...`) for beats before reveals, periods between single words for staccato ("No. More. Yawning.").
- **Each line should suggest a picture.** If you can't say what the shot is, the line is doing too little work.
- **End with a button.** The final line is dry and slightly sad or absurd, and refers back.

## Picking the rule (the whole video depends on it)
A good rule is:
- **Physical and visible:** day/night flips, gravity flips, people shrink, colors change, everything freezes, the town floods, everyone swaps bodies. The engine shows lighting changes, scale pops, crowds, weather-like moods and icons very well.
- **Triggered by an everyday action** everyone does: yawning, sneezing, clapping, laughing, blinking, saying a word, hiccuping, lying, checking your phone.
- **Exploitable** by one clever or lazy character, and **contagious or escalating**, so it can spread to the whole town.

Idea starters (all original): when you sneeze, everyone swaps clothes / when you clap, the lights go out / when you lie, you grow a centimeter / when you laugh, gravity turns off for a second / when you hiccup, you teleport one step to the left / when someone says "actually", it starts raining / when you check your phone, you age a year / when a baby cries, all the cars honk.

## Matching beats to the engine
| beat | what works visually |
|---|---|
| rule hook | `walk` toward camera + `push`, the trigger action, `punch` on the trigger word, an instant `mood` flip |
| exploit | `medium` shot, `bulb` + `sparkle`, `smirk`, finger `point` up |
| rule of three | a different set per beat (classroom, living room, clinic, office, street), `stamp` or `sign` as the result ("CLOSED", "DISMISSED!"), `punch` + `mood` flip each time |
| turn | close shot, `shock` face, `crash` or `punch` |
| chaos | `whip` from person to person, `flicker`, `establishing` high angle, walkers, animals |
| authority | podium (`plaza` + `stage`), `low` angle, `slam` gavel + `shake`, `sign` with an icon and slash |
| tension | crowd with `strain` + `tremble`, `track-right` along the faces, a carried-over sign |
| trigger | a mundane object or setting (chalkboard text, lecture, alarm clock), slow `push` |
| payoff | crowd action at once, `mood` flip, `establishing` |
| button | `title` card ("3 YEARS LATER"), or `crash` into a deadpan face + `glint` |

## What to show the user
Before building anything, show:
1. **The premise in one line** (the rule + the twist).
2. **The script**, already split into caption chunks with highlights, grouped by shot, plus one short note per shot on what we see. Example:
   - Shot 1 · plaza · hero walks toward camera, claps → punch-in: `In this town,` / `when you` / `*clap...*`
   - Shot 2 · plaza wide, low angle, crane up · lights go out: `it turns` / `*night.*`
3. **Estimated length** (words ÷ 2.9 ≈ seconds of brisk narration).
4. Optionally 2 alternatives for the hook or the ending, if the user seems undecided.

Then ask for approval or edits. Changing a script is cheap; re-rendering is not.

## Originality
Write new stories. When the user shares a reference video, copy its *format* (structure, pacing, camera language, caption style), never its script, characters or jokes. Don't use real brands, logos or real people. Keep it light: no gore, no mean-spirited punching down.
