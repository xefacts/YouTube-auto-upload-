A fly buzzes past your ear. You don't look. You just... know. Leo fumbles his phone over the sink, and before it has dropped ten centimeters, your hand is already there. Then the soccer ball leaves his foot. You watch it... all the way. Like it's swimming through honey. And your tea, sitting on the counter? Still hot.

Most people react in about a quarter of a second. Two hundred and fifty milliseconds. Eye, to brain, to hand. You? Two and a half milliseconds. Here's the rule that runs the whole story. Your brain got faster. Nothing else did. Muscles can't move quicker than muscle moves. The world didn't slow down. You just... started earlier.

So what if your reaction time was a hundred times faster? Plus: you never drop anything. Ever. Falling keys. Falling eggs. Falling grandma. Plus: video games become ridiculous. A screen refreshes sixty times a second. You see things seventeen times before anyone else does. Leo spins the controller. "Mate. You really have to stop doing that." Minus: everything else is slow. A sentence takes a whole second... and you've already written four replies. And at night, you can't switch it off. You lie there... and count.

Minus: your brain is now the most expensive organ you own. It already burns about twenty percent of your daily energy. Speed costs fuel. Your breakfast... never ends. And your brain is built on surprise. Predict everything... and nothing surprises you anymore. Leo tells a joke. You already saw the punchline in his shoulder. So you laugh... a second late. On purpose. Sad, isn't it?

Then there's Mia. Your little sister. She's nine. And she's the only person who can slow you down. Every night she needs the same thing. Two knocks on the frame of her door. Then one. That's the deal. That's the language.

Then comes the museum. A lamp goes out. Emergency lights, red and thin. Mia is across the hall, near the dark stairs. A forty kilo barrier is sliding toward a group of smaller kids. A screen falls from three meters. Eight tenths of a second. You cannot run faster. You can only start earlier.

You shout it on the metal rail. Two knocks. Then one. Mia freezes. She knows that knock. You lean into the barrier before it is really moving... and the lean wins. The screen lands on an empty patch of floor. Your sister's hand... is in yours.

That night, she knocks twice. Then once. You answer... at normal speed. The gift didn't disappear. It just stopped mattering as much. You've started letting the tea go cold... on purpose.
